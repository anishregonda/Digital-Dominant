DFW HVAC Website Report 2026

We ran the homepages of 56 HVAC companies across Dallas-Fort Worth through Google’s mobile speed test and checked each page by hand. The median site scores 55 out of 100, takes 9.8 seconds to show its main content on a phone, and weighs 3.7 MB. Four of the 56 pass.

The headline numbers

Google calls a mobile score of 90 or above “good” and below 50 “poor”. Most of DFW sits in between, and a third sits below the line.

Google’s own research found that 53% of mobile visits are likely to be abandoned when a page takes longer than 3 seconds to load. Seven in ten DFW HVAC homepages are past that mark before the first word appears.

Where the 56 sites land

The bulk of the market sits in the 40 to 69 range: working sites that are simply heavy. The four that pass are not the biggest companies. Three of them keep the homepage to about 1 MB or less; the fourth is heavier but shows its main content in 1.1 seconds.

What is on the page

The basics most owners worry about are there: the phone, financing, emergency service. The gaps are the things that turn a visitor into a booked call. Of the 20 contact forms we could count, the median asks 5 to 6 questions and half ask 6 or more. In a metro where about one in three residents speaks a language other than English at home, two sites offer Spanish.

Three findings that matter

  1. The companies paying for ads have the slowest sites.

    23 of the 56 homepages carry Google Ads conversion tracking, which means the owner is buying clicks right now. Those 23 average a mobile score of 42, against 56 for the group as a whole, and 16 of them score under 50. The money is being spent on the pages least able to turn a click into a call.

  2. Chat widgets cost about 11 points.

    The 11 sites running a chat or lead-capture widget average 47; the 45 without one average 58. The widget loads before the page does, and on a phone the visitor sees a spinner, not a phone number.

  3. The platform is not the problem.

    37 of the 56 sites run WordPress, most with Elementor or Divi. They average 57; the rest average 54. What separates a 95 from a 25 is weight, not the tool: the median homepage here is 3.7 MB, and three of the four passing sites are about 1 MB or less. Oversized hero images, video backgrounds and a dozen plugins do the damage, on any platform.

Two smaller numbers from the same data. 16 of the 56 sites use call tracking, so 13 of the 23 owners buying Google Ads can’t tell which campaign made the phone ring. And 18 run a Meta pixel, so the owner could retarget visitors who didn’t call, but most of those sites have no page worth sending them back to.

If your site is on the list

Five fixes, in the order they pay back. The first three take an afternoon.

  1. Shrink the images. Export every photo under 200 KB and drop the video background. This alone moves most sites from the 40s into the 70s.
  2. Remove what you don’t use. Plugins, sliders, fonts, the chat widget nobody answers. Every one loads before your phone number does.
  3. Make the number a button. A tap-to-call link, pinned to the bottom of the screen on mobile. 10 of 54 sites still make the visitor copy the number.
  4. Put a price range on the page. 23 of 54 show one. A visitor who sees “repairs from $89, replacements from $6,500 with financing” calls; one who sees nothing calls the competitor who shows it.
  5. Add a Spanish page. Two of the 54 sites we checked have one. For a few hundred dollars of translation, it’s the least crowded space in DFW HVAC marketing.

Method and sources

  • Sample: 56 HVAC companies across Dallas, Fort Worth, Arlington, Plano, Frisco, McKinney, Irving, Garland, Denton, Grand Prairie, Richardson, Carrollton and Mesquite, drawn from Google Maps listings with at least 50 reviews. Franchise locations of national brands were left out.
  • Speed: the Google PageSpeed Insights API, mobile strategy, Lighthouse lab data, homepage only, run on 22 October 2026. Scores move a few points between runs; the bands and medians hold.
  • Google’s bars: Lighthouse scoring (90 to 100 good, 50 to 89 needs improvement, under 50 poor), first content in 1.8 s or less and main content in 2.5 s or less.
  • Page checks: each homepage reviewed by hand on 22 October 2026 for a tap-to-call link, a visible phone number, a contact form and its number of fields, reviews, online booking, 24/7 wording, financing, a Spanish option and any price shown. Two homepages couldn’t be fully read, so those counts are out of 54.
  • Technology: WordPress, page builders, Google Ads conversion tracking, the Meta pixel, call tracking and chat widgets were identified from the network requests each homepage made during the test.
  • Abandonment: Google, The Need for Mobile Speed (2016): 53% of mobile visits are likely to be abandoned when a page takes longer than 3 seconds to load.
  • Language: U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey 2024 1-year estimates via Census Reporter: 32.8% of people in the Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington metro speak a language other than English at home.
